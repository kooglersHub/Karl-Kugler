## Giving measurements a place

A tactile reading becomes more useful when you can locate it on the hand that produced it. HaptIQ connects joint feedback and tactile measurements from an Inspire Robots RH56DFTP to an articulated browser viewer.

The application brings live inspection, recorded grasp replay, and CAD-based sensor estimation into a shared workspace. Its passive receiver observes telemetry; physical motion belongs to a separate application.

## From incoming data to an inspectable scene

The pipeline separates acquisition from interpretation. A Modbus receiver reads measurements and publishes them to a local HTTP server. The viewer can follow live readings or open a recording without connected hardware.

Recorded poses provide a way to inspect individual moments and reconstruct accumulated contacts. A frozen measurement snapshot gives the estimator a defined input to work against, rather than a moving target.

## Decisions that matter

### Keep observation separate from control

Inspecting telemetry should not silently move hardware. Normal receiver startup does not send motion, calibration, or configuration writes. Manual poses change the virtual hand.

### Freeze the evidence

Sensor placement is estimated against a selected snapshot. This keeps the relationship between a measurement, a candidate model, and its fit report inspectable.

### Keep ambiguity visible

A promising alignment is not the same thing as a confirmed sensor identity. The estimator compares contact positions, normals, hand limits, and visibility; similar fits retain ambiguity instead of becoming a false certainty.

## What the viewer can show

- Articulated hand geometry and tactile contact fields.
- Incoming joint feedback and tactile readings.
- Recorded grasps, individual poses, and reconstructed contacts.
- Candidate CAD placements and detailed fit reports.

## Limits and provenance

Geometry and taxel placement remain **approximate and uncalibrated**. Open meshes, sampled motion coverage, and finite search all limit physical interpretation. The cover shows synthetic test data and a provisional placement.

HaptIQ Hand derives from the [original HaptIQ project](https://github.com/rht2001/haptiq). This case study describes the maintained application; it does not claim that every part was created from scratch. Hand geometry, sensor CAD, and reference materials retain their upstream terms.
