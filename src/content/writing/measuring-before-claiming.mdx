A hand model can look perfectly aligned with a sensor. A digital organism can appear to seek food. Both pictures invite an explanation before the evidence is ready.

The useful next step is to ask what the picture actually establishes.

## Distinguish the states

In HaptIQ Hand, a candidate placement, a promising alignment, and a confirmed identity are different outcomes. Approximate geometry and uncalibrated taxel positions limit what a fit can support. Rendering a clean scene does not remove those limits.

The same discipline matters in an artificial-life experiment. A neural controller changing does not establish that it improved. A population surviving one run does not show that its inherited controller caused the survival.

## Preserve a way to disagree

A frozen measurement snapshot makes an estimate easier to inspect. A matched control group makes an apparent improvement easier to challenge. Both approaches preserve the conditions under which a claim could turn out to be wrong.

For a signal experiment, even the control needs care. Muting reception while retaining the energy cost of sending asks a different question from removing the channel and its costs altogether.

## Make the limit part of the interface

Uncertainty should appear where a result is interpreted. “Provisional fit” belongs next to the candidate, not only in a distant document. Unknown ancestry should stay unknown in the organism inspector.

An honest interface can still be visually clear. It just needs to distinguish observation, interpretation, and confirmation.

## Project context

These distinctions follow the documented behavior and limitations of [HaptIQ Hand](/projects/haptiq-hand/) and [EvoLab](/projects/evolab/). This is a design note, not a new report of measured experimental results.
